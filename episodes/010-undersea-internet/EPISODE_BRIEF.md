## Scene arc (match speech)
1. **0–5s:** phone sends message New York → London, imagined satellite path freezes, camera dives beneath the ocean to a glowing cable.
2. Wi-Fi radio is only the local first hop; physical routers/fiber network beyond.
3. Fiber optic strands inside protective ocean cable, digital information carried by modulated light; DON'T show photons repeatedly leaving fiber.
4. Deep-water cable roughly garden-hose thickness depending on construction, and extensive global continental network.
5. Undersea fiber vs satellite capacity/economics; satellites valuable complementary technologies, not useless.
6. Failure: anchor or fishing gear damages cable; data reroutes where alternate capacity exists; places with few alternatives face outages.
7. Huge Earth orbit pullback reveals physical ocean cable skeleton; `THE INTERNET HAS A SKELETON`.

## Guardrails
- Avoid unsupported exact global percentage (e.g. 99%) or made-up number of currently active cables.
- The route of a particular single packet is not necessarily known; say likely, not certainly.
- Fiber carries modulated light inside glass waveguides, not an empty glowing pipe.
- Cable structure and size vary with depth and protection.
- Do not depict arbitrary sabotage scenarios as documented events.

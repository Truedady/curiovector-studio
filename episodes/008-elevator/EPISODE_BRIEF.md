## Scene arc (align to audio)
1. **0–5s:** supporting cable snapping in a **hypothetical** scenario; camera drops with the car, then sudden stop-frame.
2. Explain ordinary traction elevators have multiple steel suspension ropes and redundant protection; one cable failure doesn't automatically cause free fall.
3. Reveal speed governor: separate governor rope moves through wheel; no response at normal speed, centrifugal governor trips past threshold.
4. Mechanical safety brake/wedges grip **guide rails** on both sides to halt car; show physically plausible force/contact path, not a magical brake squeezing main hoist rope.
5. Additional brake systems, including machine brakes on loss of power, distinguished from overspeed governor.
6. 1854 Elisha Otis safety demonstration: historically inspired stage, supporting rope cut, platform drops slightly and catches; avoid misleading modern elevator specs for the event.
7. Return to modern skyline: `THE SECRET IS STOPPING`.

## Science guardrails
- Technical mechanisms vary by elevator type; frame as conventional **traction elevator**.
- A severed single rope is not automatic free fall; the multiple-rope redundancy is central.
- Do not promise no elevator can ever fall or every elevator uses identical safety mechanisms.
- Governor/safety mechanical actuation should not be confused with a standard service brake.
- Historical exhibition is 1854, New York.
